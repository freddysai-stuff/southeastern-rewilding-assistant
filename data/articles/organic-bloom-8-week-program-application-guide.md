# 8-Week Organic Bloom Program — Application Schedule & Dilution Guide

An 8-week rotating soil-drench schedule (mid-June through early August, timed
to coastal Georgia's peak growing season) alternating Fish-Bone Tea and Kelp
Tea, with rest days/weeks built in so soil biology and root systems aren't
overwhelmed.

## Week-by-week schedule

| Week | Dates | Applications | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jun 15-21 | Day 1: Fish-Bone Tea (1:15) · Day 4: plain water | Establish baseline; observe response |
| 2 | Jun 22-28 | Day 1: Kelp Tea (1:15) · Day 4: plain water | Encourage new bud formation |
| 3 | Jun 29-Jul 5 | Day 1: Fish-Bone Tea · Day 4: Kelp Tea | Double-application week; monitor leaf color |
| 4 | Jul 6-12 | Plain water both days | Rest week — let soil biology reset |
| 5 | Jul 13-19 | Day 1: Fish-Bone Tea · Day 4: Kelp Tea | Peak bloom push |
| 6 | Jul 20-26 | Day 1: Kelp Tea · Day 4: Fish-Bone Tea | Order alternated for variety |
| 7 | Jul 27-Aug 2 | Day 1: Fish-Bone Tea · Day 4: Kelp Tea | Final heavy-feed week |
| 8 | Aug 3-9 | Plain water both days | Taper off; transition to maintenance |

## General application rules

- **Pre-water the day before** every tea application so the soil is
  thoroughly moist — applying concentrated nutrients to dry root zones
  causes uptake shock.
- **Time applications for early morning (before 9 AM) or late afternoon
  (after 5 PM)** to avoid heat-related stress; midday application in
  coastal summer heat stresses plants and accelerates evaporation.
- **Start dilute.** Use a 1:15 ratio for the first two weeks before moving to
  standard strength, so you can gauge each plant's individual response.
- **Watch for leaf signals.** Yellowing, curling, or brown-tipped edges
  after an application mean over-concentration or heat stress — dilute
  further next round, not a sign the program has failed.
- **Never apply to a stressed plant.** Rehydrate wilting/drought-stressed
  plants with two rounds of plain water over 24 hours first, then resume the
  schedule on the next scheduled day.

## Storage

Label each batch with its brew date. Refrigerated concentrate keeps up to 2
weeks; freeze in ice cube trays/small portions for up to 3 months. Discard
any batch that smells sour or rotten rather than earthy-fishy/oceanic — that
indicates bacterial spoilage, not normal fermentation.
