# Annual Organic Lawn Care Rotation — Brunswick, GA (Zone 9a)

This is the practical, week-to-week execution rhythm for the strategy laid
out in `chemlawn-recovery-soil-restoration-blueprint` and
`multi-year-chemlawn-recovery-transition-plan`. It rotates six cheap DIY
inputs — alfalfa tea, molasses, Epsom salt foliar, fish bone meal tea,
compost (tea and topdress), and biochar — on a repeating schedule tied to
season, not a fixed calendar date, so it holds year over year rather than
just for one specific year.

See the individual fertilizer recipes for exact mixing ratios:
`alfalfa-tea-soil-drench`, `molasses-maintenance-drench`,
`epsom-salt-foliar-spray`, `fish-bone-meal-tea-lawn-drench`,
`compost-tea-aact`, `compost-topdress`, `biochar-maintenance-broadcast`.

## Seasonal program phases

| Phase | Approx. months (Brunswick, GA) | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Peak Growing Season | Early-mid June | Full program launch — all six inputs in rotation |
| Heat Watch | Mid-June through August | Scaled-back inputs; heat-wave protocol governs |
| Recovery & Soil Building | Late August-September | Rebuild reserves; prep for clover overseed |
| Dormant Rest | Late October-January | Minimal inputs; lawn and soil rest |
| Pre-Emergence Prep | February | Light inputs only if soil/air is warm; gather materials |
| Growing Season Launch | March-May | Full program resumes as soil temps climb |

## Typical weekly rotation (Peak Growing / Recovery / Launch phases)

A representative two-week cycle during active-growth phases:

- **Week A, early in week (e.g. Monday):** Alfalfa tea soil drench, often
  combined with molasses (mixed into the same brew).
- **Week A, later in week (e.g. Thursday):** Epsom salt foliar spray
  (evening only).
- **Week B, early in week:** Fish bone meal tea soil drench.
- **Week B, midweek/weekend:** Compost - alternate between a full topdress
  (spring and fall) and a spot topdress on thin patches; blend in biochar a
  few times per season, always charged with alfalfa tea or compost first.

Rest weeks with no inputs are normal and expected during dormancy (roughly
mid-November through January) and can also occur during the deepest heat of
summer if a heat wave is active.

## Heat wave protocol (95F+ for 3+ days)

- **Continue:** Epsom salt (evening only), diluted molasses drench.
- **Pause:** Alfalfa tea, fish bone meal tea — resume once temps drop below
  90F.
- **Never during a heat wave:** Compost topdress or biochar broadcast.
- Water deeply at dawn, increasing to about 1.5 inches/week; the extra
  diluted molasses helps the grass cope with stress.

## General timing rules

- Apply liquid inputs early morning (before 9 AM) or evening (after 6 PM);
  never foliar spray in direct midday sun.
- Apply liquids 1-2 hours before light-to-moderate expected rain for best
  absorption; skip all liquid applications ahead of heavy rain (over 1 inch)
  and wait 24-48 hours after a heavy rain before a compost topdress.
- Pre-water 30 minutes before any liquid application if the soil has been
  dry for 3+ days.
- Always charge biochar with alfalfa tea or compost before broadcasting it.

## Overseeding / planting windows

- **Millet & Sorghum:** overseed April-June once soil is 65F+. This refines
  the more general late-spring (May-June) guidance in
  `browntop-millet-partridge-pea-unmowed-pollinator-plot` with a soil-temperature
  trigger rather than a fixed month — start as early as April if soil has
  already warmed.
- **Sunflowers:** direct sow March-May, with a fall succession planting in
  September.
- **Clover (White Dutch / Crimson):** best overseed window Sept 15-Oct 15.
  `winter-clover-choices-crimson-carolina-white-dutch` gives a slightly wider
  October-November sowing window for the same fall-only rule — treat the
  full practical window as **mid-September through early November**: earlier
  (mid-Sept) sowing gives clover more time to establish before the first
  frost, while sowing into October/November still works, just with less
  margin. Both sources agree fall sowing only — never spring.
- **Winter Rye (cover crop):** overseed October 1-November 1.
- **Prep for any overseed:** scalp mow low, rake the surface, broadcast
  seed, add a light compost cover, and keep consistently moist for the first
  10 days.

## A note on zone labeling

This calendar labels the property **Zone 9a**; some earlier-ingested docs
(`seasonal/zone-8a-spring`, `seasonal/zone-8a-fall`) use **8a**, while several
chemlawn-recovery articles tag **zone-8b-9a**. Brunswick, GA sits right on
the 8b/9a boundary depending on the exact USDA map revision used, so this
isn't a real contradiction in growing advice — but the `seasonal/*.json`
zone labels should eventually be reconciled to a single consistent zone
string (recommend 8b/9a or 9a) rather than left as 8a.
