# Summer/Fall Companion Planting for North-Facing Shade Beds (Brunswick, GA)

Non-legumes are "nitrogen scavengers" that rapidly build root mass and
organic matter; legumes are "nitrogen fixers" that pull nitrogen from the
air. Pairing one of each per season maximizes root diversity and balances
the carbon-to-nitrogen ratio for a north-facing bed in coastal Georgia
(zip 31525-equivalent, Zone 8b/9a).

## Non-legume base options

**Summer (plant in June - heat & shade tolerant biomass builders):**
- **Japanese Millet** (*Echinochloa esculenta*) - unlike most summer
  millets, tolerates partial shade and wet, poorly-drained soil; fast
  growth, massive root system breaks up compaction.
- **Coleus** (Sprengeri/*Solenostemon*) - ornamental, shade-loving, dense
  fibrous roots keep soil loose.
- **Sweet Potato Vine** (*Ipomoea batatas*) - fast, heat-tolerant living
  mulch; smothers summer weeds, cools soil surface.

**Fall (overseed October-November - cool-season builders):**
- **Annual Ryegrass** (*Lolium multiflorum*) - most shade-tolerant
  cool-season cover grass available for Southern beds; dense fibrous root
  web.
- **Daikon/Tillage Radish** (*Raphanus sativus* var. *longipinnatus*) -
  biological drill; taproot punches through compaction, then rots in place
  leaving air/water channels.
- **Cereal Rye** (*Secale cereale*) - hardy, moderately shade-tolerant;
  scavenges nutrients so they don't leach over winter.

## Legume pairings

| Pair with | Legume partner | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Japanese Millet | Iron & Clay Cowpeas (*Vigna unguiculata*) | One of the few summer legumes tolerant of partial shade; millet acts as a living trellis, cowpeas fix nitrogen into its root zone |
| Sweet Potato Vine / Coleus | Partridge Pea (*Chamaecrista fasciculata*) | Native Southern annual that naturally grows in pine-woods edge habitat; bright yellow blooms elevate an ornamental bed while fixing nitrogen (same plant used for unmowed pollinator plots in browntop-millet-partridge-pea-unmowed-pollinator-plot - safe here too since flower beds aren't mowed) |
| Annual Ryegrass / Cereal Rye | Crimson Clover (*Trifolium incarnatum*) | Gold-standard Southern winter cover; rye grows upright, clover blankets beneath, erupts in deep-crimson blooms in early spring |
| Daikon Radish | Hairy Vetch (*Vicia villosa*) | Radish drills a vertical taproot, vetch carpets horizontally and supplies the nitrogen the hungry radish needs |

**Suggested blend ratios (by weight):**
- Summer mix: 60% Japanese Millet + 40% Iron & Clay Cowpeas.
- Fall mix: 70% Annual Ryegrass/Cereal Rye + 30% Crimson Clover, **or** 70%
  Daikon Radish + 30% Hairy Vetch.

**Inoculant reminder:** coat legume seed with the correct live rhizobia
inoculant right before planting (Cowpea inoculant for the summer mix;
Clover/Vetch inoculant for the fall mix) or it won't fix nitrogen.

## Worked example: 3 beds, 30' x 2' (180 sq ft total), against a north wall

- **June:** Broadcast 2 oz Outsidepride Partial Shade Wildflower Mix
  (covers up to 250 sq ft) across all three beds for immediate color and
  light soil action; tuck a few Iron & Clay Cowpea seeds every 2 ft along
  the back edge against the house to climb the foundation and add nitrogen.
- **Mid-October:** Hand-shear/weed-eat the bed down to ~2 in, leaving
  clippings in place as mulch.
- **Late October:** Broadcast 1 lb Outsidepride Gardenway Cover Crop Seed
  Mix (triticale, wheat, Austrian field pea, common vetch, crimson clover,
  annual ryegrass - a cool-season-only blend, dies off above 85-90F, not
  suitable for June planting) directly over the residue and water in
  heavily; it stays green through winter and blooms red/purple by
  late-Feb/March.
- No pre-packaged mix currently covers deep-shade + summer-heat together -
  build a custom summer blend from individual seed packs (as above), or use
  a shade-rated ornamental wildflower/turf mix, and save pre-packaged
  cool-season mixes for the fall overseed.
