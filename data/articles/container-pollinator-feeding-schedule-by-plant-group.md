# Feeding a Mixed Potted Pollinator Collection — Grouping Heavy, Moderate, and Lean Feeders

A single feeding schedule doesn't work across a mixed pollinator container
collection - native prairie species and tropical exotics have opposite
nutrient tolerances, and matching the wrong group to a rich feeding
schedule causes weak, floppy growth or missed blooms. Group plants into
three feeding tiers (using the miracle-gro-bloom-booster-10-52-10 recipe,
1 tbsp per gallon of water applied directly to soil):

**Group 1 - Heavy feeders (every 2 weeks)**: Golden Shrimp Plant (Justicia
brandegeeana) and Cuphea (Mexican Heather/Cigar Plant) - non-stop tropical
bloomers that burn through container nutrients fast.

**Group 2 - Moderate feeders (every 3-4 weeks)**: Agastache 'Blue Blaze'
(hummingbird favorite, can get top-heavy if overfed) and Black-eyed Susan
(Rudbeckia hirta) - container growth strips their nutrients faster than an
in-ground bed, so a light monthly dose keeps them vibrant without pushing
weak growth.

**Group 3 - Lean-soil prairie natives (every 6 weeks max)**: Gaillardia
(Blanket Flower) and Coneflower (Echinacea purpurea) - genetically adapted
to rocky, nutrient-starved dirt. Overfeeding makes their stems weak and
"leggy." A single light dose mid-summer is usually enough.

## Keeping it safe for pollinators

- Apply liquid fertilizer strictly to the soil, never sprayed over leaves
  or open petals - hummingbirds and bees drink nectar directly from flower
  tubes, and this keeps chemical salts out of their food source.
- Feed in the evening after pollinator activity has stopped for the day, so
  the soil fully absorbs the nutrients before bees/hummingbirds return the
  next morning.
- Always water plain first if soil is dry, then apply the fertilizer mix -
  this single step prevents most fertilizer-related plant deaths from
  root/salt burn.
