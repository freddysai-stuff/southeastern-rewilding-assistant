# Free Plant Propagation from a Container Collection — Seed Collection vs. Pulling Plugs

A mixed pollinator container collection can be expanded for free using two
different techniques depending on how each plant grows - trying to use one
method on the wrong plant type wastes a season.

## Method 1: Collecting seeds (best for single-crown perennials)

Instead of deadheading every spent bloom, let the final flowers of the
season mature into seed heads.

- **Black-eyed Susan & Coneflower**: let petals drop until the center cone
  turns hard, dry, and dark brown/black. Cut the cone into a paper bag,
  shake vigorously, and the seeds fall free.
- **Gaillardia (Blanket Flower)**: flowers form round, prickly seed balls
  after petals fade. Once brown and dry, crumble between fingers to
  collect.
- Store dried seed in paper envelopes in a cool, dark spot; sow the
  following spring.

## Method 2: Pulling plugs (best for clumping or spreading plants)

"Pulling a plug" means dividing a rooted piece of the plant - roots, soil,
and all - directly out of a container to plant elsewhere.

- **Agastache & Black-eyed Susan**: these send up new stems from the root
  crown, forming a tight cluster. Slide the whole plant from its pot the
  following spring and use a sharp knife to slice the root ball into 2-3
  separate plugs, each with its own leaves and roots.
- **Spreading/trailing groundcovers** (see the Blue Daze layering
  propagation doc) root wherever a stem node touches soil and can be tugged
  free as a pre-rooted plug without disturbing the main plant.

## One important warning

Do not use a high-phosphorus bloom-booster fertilizer on any plant being
grown specifically as a "seed mother" for the season - concentrated
phosphorus pushes bloom size/speed but can reduce overall seed viability
and germination rate. Stick to a slow-release organic granular feed on
seed-collection plants instead.
