# Multi-Year Soil Recovery Plan — From Chemical Sand to Permanent Groundcover

A phased, multi-year framework for turning chemically dead, compacted sandy
soil into self-sustaining ground cover, built around cheap DIY inputs and
low code-enforcement visibility (see stealth-lawn-code-compliance-strategy).

## Phase 1 — Soil cleansing & microbe activation (early summer, day 1)

Apply a biochar + dish-soap + molasses drench (see the biochar/soap/molasses
detox recipe) on an overcast day with rain forecast that evening. Then let
the soil rest 3-5 dry, warm days ("the cook window") before planting — this
lets soap strip water-repelling waxes off sand grains, biochar lock up
lingering herbicide residue, and molasses sugar spike native bacteria
populations.

## Phase 2 — Summer planting (day 5-6 after detox)

- **Main lawn areas:** broadcast a millet + low-profile legume mix (e.g.
  Browntop Millet + Alyceclover), lightly raked in ~1/2" deep. Mow a low
  border and walking paths through the interior as it grows. For lawns with
  30+ years of continuous chemical treatment and especially severe
  compaction, see `two-year-aggressive-decompaction-cover-crop-plan` for a
  more aggressive alternative (Sorghum-Sudangrass + Daikon Radish, etc.)
  built around freshly-made, charged biochar.
- **Sunny flower beds:** Sunn Hemp, seeded 1/2-1" deep (see sunn-hemp vs
  buckwheat article for details), working around any spiky succulents.
- **North-facing shade beds:** Hairy Vetch, broadcast and kept damp until
  established.

## Phase 3 — Autumn termination & winter hand-off (late Sept/early Oct)

Mow all summer crops flat, leaving the green debris in place as mulch, then
overseed directly into the chopped residue:

- Main lawn: pre-inoculated Crimson Clover.
- Sunny flower beds: White Dutch Clover (permanent, only sow in fall — see
  winter-clover-choices article).
- North shade beds: Subterranean Clover (self-seeding).

## Phase 4 — Spring re-entry and beyond (year 2+)

Once winter clover blooms and fades in spring, mow it down and decide how to
proceed for the next summer:

1. **Repeat & build (free)**: replant leftover summer cover crop seed from
   the original bulk purchase — costs nothing and continues adding organic
   carbon. Recommended for at least a second full year before switching to
   a permanent turf, since one season alone rarely fully reverses decades of
   chemical compaction.
2. **"Lazy man's lawn" (e.g. Centipede Grass)**: once soil is no longer
   hydrophobic and holds moisture, slow-growing, low-input turf species that
   would have failed on the original dead sand become viable.
3. **Drought-bulletproof turf (e.g. Bahiagrass)**: a deep, massive root
   system for a "never baby it again" permanent lawn once soil structure has
   recovered.

By giving the soil two full growing seasons of cover-crop root activity
before committing to permanent turf, the sand has time to become genuinely
rich, water-retentive loam rather than risking a costly turf failure on
soil that isn't ready yet.
