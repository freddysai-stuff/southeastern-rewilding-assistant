# Shade-Tolerant Flowering Legumes for Rejuvenating North-Facing Beds

Standard sun-loving agricultural legumes (Sunn Hemp, cowpeas) struggle or
grow leggy in a north-facing bed's limited direct light. This is a
comparison of ornamental, shade-tolerant flowering legumes suited to that
setting - a broader "which legume should I plant" reference than the
permanent shrub/perennial list in `shade-soil-rebuilders-north-facing-beds`
(Baptisia, Amorpha, Alsike Clover, Erythrina) or the specific rotation in
`north-shade-bed-cover-crop-rotation-vetch-subclover` (Hairy Vetch to
Subterranean Clover).

## Comparison

| Legume | Shade tolerance | Flower color | Primary soil benefit | Life cycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red Clover (*Trifolium pratense*) | High (for a legume) | Pink/purple | Loosens clay, breaks compaction via deep taproot | Perennial/biennial |
| Hairy Vetch (*Vicia villosa*) | High | Purple/blue | Massive nitrogen + weed suppression via dense vining mat | Winter annual |
| Fava/Broad Beans (*Vicia faba*) | Moderate-high | White & black/purple | Huge succulent green biomass for chop-and-drop | Cool-season annual |
| Austrian Winter Pea (*Pisum sativum* var. *arvense*) | Moderate | Two-tone pink/purple | Quick nitrogen release, fibrous roots improve tilth | Cool-season annual |
| Wild Lupine (*Lupinus perennis*) | Moderate (part shade) | Blue/purple/pink | Deep nitrogen fixing, permanent ornamental structure | Perennial |

Note: Hairy Vetch appears here and in the vetch-to-subterranean-clover
rotation article - same plant, different use case (a broad ornamental
legume option here vs. a specific managed summer/winter rotation there).

## Strategic tips

- **Always use the correct inoculant.** Legumes need a symbiotic
  relationship with Rhizobium bacteria to fix nitrogen - buy pre-inoculated
  seed or a localized soil inoculant (Pea/Vetch-specific or Clover-specific)
  to get the full nutrient return. Without it, legumes simply drain
  nitrogen from soil rather than fixing it from the air.
- **Chop and drop at peak flowering**, not after drying out or by pulling
  roots. Nitrogen levels in plant tissue peak right as flowering begins; cut
  at the base, leave roots to rot in place (opening air channels), and lay
  the tops down as mulch. This matches the general chop-and-drop principle
  in `using-weeds-as-garden-inputs-overview`.
- **Supplement with non-legume biomass.** Shady, depleted beds often have
  poor drainage and low microbial activity - mixing in a shade-tolerant
  grass (fine fescue or oats) keeps soil biology active while the legumes
  fix nitrogen.
