# Browntop Millet + Partridge Pea Mix for an Unmowed Pollinator Plot

Combining Browntop Millet and native Partridge Pea works very well as an
**unmowed summer wildflower/pollinator plot** — a different use case from a
regularly mowed "stealth lawn."

> **Important distinction**: this mix is for a plot left to grow and bloom
> through the season. If your goal is a regularly mowed low-profile lawn
> (see summer-cover-crop-comparison-millet-cowpeas-peanut and the
> stealth-lawn strategy article), do **not** add Partridge Pea — its
> upright, stiff-stemmed habit means mowing at lawn height removes the
> flower buds before it can bloom and reseed, wasting the seed investment.
> Alyceclover is the better mower-tolerant legume partner for a mowed lawn.

## Why the mix works for an unmowed plot

- **Synergy**: Millet acts as a fast-growing nurse crop, providing physical
  support and light shade; Partridge Pea fixes nitrogen that feeds the
  nitrogen-hungry millet in return.
- **Visual contrast**: bright yellow Partridge Pea blossoms and fern-like
  foliage weave through the coarser, golden-green millet seed heads.
- **Pollinator value**: millet provides structure and bird feed; Partridge
  Pea's extrafloral nectaries draw large numbers of native bees, butterflies,
  and beneficial predatory insects.

## Seeding & management

- Reduce the standard millet seeding rate by ~30-50% so the grass doesn't
  choke out the Partridge Pea. Target roughly **15-20 lb Browntop Millet +
  6-10 lb Partridge Pea per acre**.
- Broadcast together into a clean, raked seedbed in late spring (May-June)
  once soil is thoroughly warm. Scratch in ~1/4-1/2 inch deep and press
  firmly.
- Partridge Pea flowers heavily from July until first frost — avoid mowing
  the plot until late summer/early fall once pods have fully matured, to
  get the maximum bloom display and allow self-seeding for next year.
