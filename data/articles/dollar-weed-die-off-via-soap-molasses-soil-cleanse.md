# Dollar Weed Die-Off & Centipede Recovery After a Soap/Molasses Soil Cleanse

A real-world confirmation of what the `biochar-soap-molasses-soil-detox`
recipe is designed to do: within days of a dish soap + molasses soil
cleanse (followed by mechanical mowing/scalping), established Dollar Weed
died back and existing Centipede grass patches began actively recovering -
on soil that had received 2,4-D herbicide roughly three months earlier.

## Why Dollar Weed turns brown after the cleanse

Dollar Weed thrives specifically in compacted, stagnant, low-oxygen dirt.
The soap dissolves the waxy protective coating on its leaves, which lets
the molasses and its sugars alter the soil's surface environment - breaking
surface tension and waking up aerobic microbes. That combination makes
previously hospitable compacted soil actively hostile to Dollar Weed, and
it starves out: remaining leaves turn brown and die rather than resprouting.
Mechanically scalping/mowing off the waxy leaves afterward is a useful
follow-up knockout punch on top of the chemical/bio cleanse.

## Why Centipede grass recovers at the same time

Centipede loves acidic, sandy coastal soil but suffocates under long-term
synthetic chemical compaction. The same soap treatment that loosens sand
for the cover crop planting also frees up Centipede's root zone, and the
molasses provides a clean carbon energy source - together producing a
visible growth spurt in surviving Centipede patches within the same window
as the Dollar Weed die-off.

## A caution about the "quiet" period

Immediately after a soap/molasses cleanse plus a recent herbicide
application, expect an unusually **weed-free** window - this is not
necessarily lawn health, it's a temporary cleared runway from the combined
chemical and bio-cleanse effects. Don't read low weed pressure in these
first few weeks as a permanent result; keep monitoring as the treated area
re-establishes.
