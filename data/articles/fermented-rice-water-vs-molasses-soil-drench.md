# Fermented Rice Water vs. Molasses Drench — Which Scales for a Full Yard?

Fermented rice water is a legitimate, well-studied soil amendment — not just
internet hype — but it doesn't scale well to a full yard-sized restoration
project.

## Why fermented rice water works

- Washing raw rice leaches roughly 7-70% of its water-soluble nutrients into
  the rinse water.
- Left to ferment 3-9 days, wild lactic acid bacteria and Bacillus species
  colonize the sugary water and multiply rapidly.
- Peer-reviewed research shows 3 days of fermentation can increase available
  nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, and zinc by up to ~56% versus
  unfermented rinse water — acting like a probiotic drench for soil.

## Why it fails at yard scale

- Properly treating thousands of square feet would require fermenting
  hundreds of pounds of rice in large containers weekly — impractical
  logistics and cost for a home DIY project.
- It remains an excellent, free option for small garden beds, containers,
  or houseplants, just not a full-yard soil-rebuilding strategy.

## The scaled-up equivalent: blackstrap molasses drench

Blackstrap molasses serves the same biological purpose — feeding native soil
bacteria a dense hit of raw sugar and trace minerals — at yard scale for a
few dollars. Mixed with water (and typically dish soap to break hydrophobic
surface tension on sandy soil) and applied via a hose-end sprayer, it feeds
the same native lactic-acid and Bacillus bacteria populations, causing them
to multiply rapidly without the logistics of fermenting rice. See the
biochar/soap/molasses soil detox recipe for exact mixing ratios.

**Rule of thumb:** save fermented rice water for potted plants and small
beds; use a molasses-based drench for anything measured in thousands of
square feet.
