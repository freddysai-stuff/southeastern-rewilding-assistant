# Keeping Cut Roses & Wild Foliage Fresh Longer — Homemade Vase Care

Woody stems (roses) and soft, hollow, feathery stems (Dog Fennel and
similar wild fillers) drink water differently, so a single technique won't
keep both fresh - combine these steps for a mixed bouquet.

## 1. Hot-water shock for hollow/feathery stems

Soft, hollow stems like Dog Fennel wilt fast because air bubbles get
trapped inside the moment they're cut. Plunge the bottom 1 in. of freshly
cut stems into very warm (near-hot) water for about 30 seconds immediately
after cutting - the heat forces trapped air out and lets the stem
rapidly draw up water once placed in the vase.

## 2. Clean angled cuts

Always cut stems (roses and wild fillers alike) at a sharp 45-degree angle,
ideally while holding the stem underwater. An angled cut prevents the flat
stem end from sitting flush against the vase floor, which would otherwise
block water intake.

## 3. Strip submerged foliage

Remove every leaf, thorn, or feathery branch that would sit below the
vase's waterline. Leaves left underwater rot quickly, breeding bacteria
that clogs stems, clouds the water, and smells bad.

## 4. Homemade vase food (per 1 quart warm water)

- 1 tbsp white sugar - provides carbohydrates to sustain blooming.
- 1 tsp household bleach - sanitizes against bacteria from wild stems.
- 1 tbsp lemon juice (or a crushed vitamin C tablet) - lowers water pH to
  match what cut stems prefer, helping them drink faster.

Change the vase water and mix a fresh batch every 2-3 days, trimming
1/4 in. off the stem bottoms each time to keep them drinking well.
