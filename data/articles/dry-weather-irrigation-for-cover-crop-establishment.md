# Establishing Cover Crop Seed During a Dry Spell (No Rain in the Forecast)

Soil-detox treatments (biochar/soap/molasses drenches) and freshly broadcast
cover crop seed both depend on rain to work — but a dry spell doesn't have to
delay the whole project if irrigation is available.

## If there's no rain and no irrigation

Do not apply a soap/molasses drench to bone-dry sand and let it sit under
full sun with no water — it will bake into a sticky, glazed crust that makes
hydrophobia (water repellency) worse, not better. Wait for a real rain
forecast before applying, or use a manual flush (below).

## Manual flush technique (with a hose, no sprinklers)

1. **Pre-dampen** the target area for ~5 minutes with a spray nozzle so
   biochar sticks to damp sand instead of blowing away.
2. **Apply the dry biochar**, then follow immediately with the soap/molasses
   liquid mix via hose-end sprayer.
3. **Manually flush** — switch to a high-volume sprinkler or wide-spray
   nozzle and simulate a 15-20 minute downpour (roughly 75-100 gallons at a
   typical 5 gallon-per-minute hose rate) to drive the soap, sugar, and
   carbon 2-3 inches into the sand.
4. **Delay seeding.** The detox mix can sit safely in dry soil for 2-3 weeks;
   don't broadcast seed until there's a real forecast for consistent
   moisture — millet and legume seed need steady dampness for the first 4-5
   germination days, and hand-watering thousands of square feet in dry heat
   is a losing battle.
5. For large areas, split the work across multiple days (e.g. one zone per
   day) rather than trying to treat everything in one session.

## Automated sprinkler-on-timer schedule (once seed is down)

For the first ~7 days after planting, short frequent bursts beat one long
soak on fast-draining sand:

- **7:00 AM (15 min)** — dampens sand before daytime heat.
- **12:00 PM (10 min)** — cooling mist at peak heat to protect shallow
  sprouts.
- **4:00 PM (15 min)** — recharges moisture through the evening.
- Avoid watering after dark — nighttime moisture in humid climates invites
  fungal disease in young seedlings.

Once seedlings sprout (~day 10), cut back to once a day (morning only); once
plants reach ~12 inches tall, irrigation can typically be turned off
entirely and left to natural rainfall/humidity.

## Wind & drainage safety note

Dry biochar is very lightweight. Apply it on a calm morning and lock it down
immediately with the liquid mix — once wetted it becomes heavy and won't
blow into pools, drains, or neighboring property during a pre-storm wind
gust.
