# Sunn Hemp vs. Buckwheat for Rebuilding Dead Flower Beds

Both are fast summer cover crops for smothering weeds and rebuilding dead,
sandy flower-bed soil, but they behave very differently.

## Comparison

| Feature | Buckwheat | Sunn Hemp |
|---|---|---|
| Water needs | High — wilts quickly if sand dries out | Low — thrives in dry sand once established |
| Seed risk | Higher — can persist/reseed as a weed if allowed to go to full seed | Very low in most of the US (tropical, generally can't set viable seed outside extreme south Florida) |
| Flowers | Small white blossoms | Showy, bright yellow orchid-like spikes |
| Soil value | Scavenges phosphorus from deep soil, releases it on top when chopped | Fixes up to ~100 lb nitrogen/acre in 45-60 days; roots produce compounds that suppress root-knot nematodes |
| Maintenance | Chop at first flower (~day 35) before it sets seed | Chop at peak bloom (~day 60) before stalks get woody |

## Sunn Hemp (Crotalaria juncea) growing notes

- **Germination**: reliable in 3 days at 65-80°F soil, no scarification or
  soaking needed. Seedlings emerge within a week.
- **Early growth is deceptive** — most of the first month is invisible
  taproot development. Rapid top growth (up to 12"/week) kicks in after the
  root system anchors, continuing to peak bloom around day 60.
- **Bury seed 1/2" to 1" deep** — unlike millet, it can't just be surface-
  raked; uncovered seed on hydrophobic sand will bake or wash away.
- **Buy pre-inoculated / coated seed** — dead or long-chemically-treated
  soil lacks the native Rhizobium bacteria legumes need; coated seed brings
  its own bacterial jacket.
- **Stop watering once true leaves emerge.** Overwatering causes rot;
  drought stress instead drives the taproot deeper, which is desirable for
  breaking up compacted layers.
- 5 lb of seed (~$15-20) comfortably covers a few hundred square feet of
  flower bed.

## Working around spiky companion plants (Aloe, Century Plant/Agave)

- Clip the sharp terminal needle tips off Agave leaves with long-handled
  loppers before working nearby — doesn't harm the plant, protects you.
- Keep any soap/detox liquid drench focused on bare sand between spiky
  plants, not directly on them.
- Scratch seeding channels ~1" deep with a heavy rake, then simply walk the
  seeded rows and press seed in with boot heels rather than raking around
  the spiky plants (a rake can bounce back off them).
- Sunn Hemp's tall, narrow upright habit visually complements architectural
  succulents — yellow blooms behind blue-gray Agave/Aloe reads as an
  intentional high-end planting rather than neglect.
