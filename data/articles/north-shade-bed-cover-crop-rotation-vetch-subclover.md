# North-Facing Shade Bed Cover Crop Rotation — Hairy Vetch to Subterranean Clover

North-facing beds (in the shadow of a house) get little direct winter sun and
tend to stay damp or bare — a common target for weeds and code complaints.
Most high-performance summer/winter cover crops (Sunn Hemp, White Dutch
Clover) need 4-6+ hours of direct sun and will stall out in true shade, so a
separate rotation works better here.

## The rotation

1. **Summer: Hairy Vetch (Vicia villosa).** Shade-tolerant, handles poor
   sandy soil, produces a dense vining mat with purple flowers. Elite
   nitrogen fixer; canopy smothers shade-loving weeds. Stays relatively low
   and trailing (12-24 in), so it doesn't read as an overgrown jungle.
2. **Chop down in late September/October** as the season turns.
3. **Winter: Subterranean Clover (Trifolium subterraneum).** Uniquely
   shade-adapted; grows strictly horizontally into a dense carpet under 3-5
   in tall. After flowering it physically pushes its own seed pods into the
   sand — self-planting, so it doesn't need to be reseeded once
   established.

## Execution notes

- Treat north beds with the same soil-detox pass as sunnier areas (see
  biochar-soap-molasses soil detox recipe) before the first planting.
- May/June: broadcast Hairy Vetch seed, keep sand damp until sprouted.
- October: chop the vetch down, broadcast Subterranean Clover directly into
  the residue; it will establish over winter into a self-sustaining shade
  groundcover.

This is a distinct approach from full-sun legume options (e.g. Baptisia,
Amorpha, Alsike Clover) — for very deep, ornamental shade beds where a
managed cover-crop rotation isn't the goal, see the ornamental
shade-tolerant nitrogen fixer article instead.
