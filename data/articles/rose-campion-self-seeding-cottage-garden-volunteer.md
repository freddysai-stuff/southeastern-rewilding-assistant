# Rose Campion — A Benign Fuzzy Volunteer Worth Keeping

## Identification

A "fuzzy weed" with soft, silvery-grey, velvety foliage that volunteers
into sunny, dry beds is very likely Rose Campion (Silene coronaria), a
classic cottage-garden self-seeder. The leaves resemble Lamb's Ear at a
glance, but it sends up tall, elegant flowering stems topped with intense
magenta-pink blooms. (If instead it has massive flannel-soft leaves and a
giant yellow flower spike, that's Mullein/Verbascum thapsus - a different
plant with similar fuzzy texture but very different flowers and size.)

## Why this one doesn't need control

Unlike aggressive spreaders such as Dog Fennel or Spanish Needles, Rose
Campion is a benign, short-lived self-seeder with no meaningful ecological
downside - there is little reason to remove volunteers unless they're
physically crowding out a desired plant. It thrives on neglect in poor, dry
soil and should not be over-fed or over-watered, which shortens its
naturally short (biennial/short-lived perennial) lifespan.

## Positive uses

- The tall magenta stems are a favorite cut-flower companion for roses in a
  vase, giving height and contrast at zero cost.
- To harvest free seed for more volunteers, let flower heads dry completely
  on the plant at the end of summer, then shake the tiny black seeds
  directly into new beds or pots the following spring.
