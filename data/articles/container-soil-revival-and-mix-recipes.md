# Reviving Old Container Soil & Building Custom Mixes by Plant Type

## Reviving last year's potting soil (50/50 method)

Old container soil is typically stripped of nutrients and compacted, but it
does not need to be thrown out.

1. Empty last year's soil into a wheelbarrow or large tub; break up hard
   clumps and remove old root balls/debris.
2. Mix the old soil 50/50 with fresh compost or aged manure - this restores
   microbes and fluffiness (drainage) that old soil loses.
3. If the soil feels heavy or clay-like, stir in a few handfuls of perlite
   or coarse sand.
4. Blend in a slow-release organic granular fertilizer (e.g., Jobe's
   Organics) for a safe nutrient foundation that won't burn roots for the
   next ~2 months.
5. A fresh, chopped legume cover crop (e.g., pre-bloom Sunn Hemp - see the
   Sunn Hemp cover crop articles) mixed into a tub of old soil and left to
   sit over winter acts as a free nitrogen "cook" that further re-energizes
   it by spring.

## Custom fabric grow-bag mixes by plant water/drainage needs

Fabric grow bags breathe and dry out faster than plastic pots, so don't use
one blanket recipe for every plant - match the amendment to the plant's
native habitat:

**Tropical heavy-drinkers** (Golden Shrimp Plant, Cuphea): 3 parts revived
soil/compost + 1 part peat moss (+ 1/2 part vermiculite if budget allows,
otherwise increase peat slightly). Peat locks in moisture the fabric would
otherwise lose too fast.

**Desert/prairie natives** (Gaillardia, Coneflower): 3 parts revived
soil/compost + 1 part perlite only. Skip peat and vermiculite entirely -
these plants are adapted to rocky, fast-draining, bone-dry ground and will
rot if kept too moist.

**Balanced perennials** (Blue Daze, Black-eyed Susan, Agastache): 2 parts
revived soil/compost + 1 part peat moss + 1 part perlite - enough moisture
retention to keep blooming without staying soggy.

## Budget shopping and prep tips

- Buy one compressed bale of sphagnum peat moss (2.2-3.0 cu ft) and one
  2 cu ft bag of horticultural perlite rather than small loose bags -
  far more economical per cubic foot.
- Compressed peat moss is bone-dry and water-repellent straight out of the
  bag. Dump the amount needed into a bin, pour a gallon of water over it,
  and work it in with a shovel/hands until it feels like a damp sponge
  before mixing with soil - dry peat mixed into dry soil is nearly
  impossible to wet evenly later.
- Mix each recipe in a large batch on a tarp or in a storage bin rather than
  bag-by-bag, then fill each plant group's containers from that batch before
  moving to the next recipe.
