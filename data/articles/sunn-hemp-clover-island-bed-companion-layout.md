# Sunn Hemp + White Dutch Clover Island Bed Layout & Germination Timeline

A worked, real-world layout for a planting island: Sunn Hemp in the center,
White Dutch Clover (DWC) wrapped around the perimeter as a living border -
also used along shade bed edges. Documented here alongside a real
germination timeline for reference when troubleshooting "is this on
schedule" questions during a lawn/bed establishment.

## Why the layout works

- **Sunn Hemp center:** shoots up like a fast-growing tropical canopy,
  eventually 4-5 ft tall, casting a wide shadow footprint across the island
  during the day.
- **Clover border:** low, spreading DWC blankets the edges as a living
  mulch - traps moisture, blocks weeds creeping in from the sides, and gets
  a genuine heat-stress advantage from the Sunn Hemp's shade once it's
  tall enough (see the summer dormancy section in
  `white-dutch-clover-behavior`).

## Germination timeline (observed, Brunswick sand, early summer)

- **Day 0-1:** seed cracks, microscopic taproot sent down to find moisture
  before afternoon heat. If seed is pre-inoculated, moisture activates live
  rhizobia bacteria, which begin latching onto new root hairs.
- **Days 1-4 (most vulnerable window):** zero heat tolerance yet - keep
  frequent automated watering running exactly as scheduled and avoid
  walking on the area; foot traffic can crush microscopic seedlings into
  damp sand.
- **"Two-leaf" phase:** cotyledons (temporary, smooth, round seed leaves)
  emerge first.
- **~72 hours after two-leaf:** first true three-leaf clover shapes unfold
  from the stem center; underground, rhizobia form tiny pinkish nodules on
  root hairs - the nitrogen-fixing factory setting up. Dense seeding shows
  as a visible "carpet" effect once two-leaf sprouts are thick enough to
  shade out the soil surface completely, locking in moisture and blocking
  buried weed seed from finding light.
- **Grains (millet/milo/sorghum) on the same lawn typically lag clover**:
  they spend their first few days working entirely underground building an
  anchor root before pushing green leaves into hot sun. Seeing cracked
  seed/white root hairs poking out is a normal, on-schedule sign - the
  visible pop-up (needle-thin lime-green millet shoots, fatter dark-green
  milo spikes) follows within another 1-2 days once the anchor root is set.
  A thin-looking broadcast area at this stage is not a failure - bushing
  out happens after root anchoring and leaf unfolding, and a wide-area
  broadcast (e.g. thousands of sq ft) will always look sparser than a
  dense, hand-thrown ribbon planting along a fence line at the same age.

## Practical takeaway

Don't rush to reseed a thin-looking area while grains are still in the
underground anchor-root phase - wait for the leaf pop-up before judging
density, and hold spare seed in reserve to spot-treat any truly bare
patches once the first flush is fully up.
