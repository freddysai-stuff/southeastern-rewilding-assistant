# Transplanting Nursery Plants Safely in Mid-Summer Heat

Nursery plants left in small pots through mid-summer are usually tightly
root-bound with roots heating up fast inside small plastic containers -
moving them up promptly prevents heat stress even though summer isn't the
"ideal" transplant season.

## Timing and method

1. Pick the right day: transplant in the evening or on a fully overcast
   day. Never transplant in blazing midday sun.
2. Slide the plant out of its nursery pot gently. If roots are wound
   tightly into a "bird's nest," tease and tear the bottom roots apart with
   your fingers to force them to grow outward into the new soil instead of
   continuing to circle.
3. Set the plant at the correct depth: the top of the root ball should sit
   level with the new soil line (burying stems too deep causes rot) -
   except for plants specifically benefiting from deeper planting for
   stability (see the Golden Shrimp Plant record).
4. Water immediately and thoroughly until it runs out the drainage holes.

## Aftercare

- Keep newly transplanted pots in dappled shade for 2-3 days to let roots
  recover before facing full summer sun.
- Wait a full week after transplanting before applying any bloom-booster
  fertilizer, to give roots time to settle first.
- Perennials with strong winter hardiness (native prairie species like
  Coneflower, Black-eyed Susan, Gaillardia, or hardy Agastache cultivars)
  can stay in outdoor containers year-round; tender tropicals (Golden
  Shrimp Plant, Cuphea) must be moved indoors or into a garage before the
  first hard frost, which is the main advantage of keeping them portable in
  pots rather than planting them in the ground.
