# "Stealth Lawn" Strategy — Rebuilding Soil Without Triggering Code Enforcement

Cover-cropping a large area for soil rebuilding can look like neglect to
neighbors or county code enforcement ("lawn police"), even when it's an
intentional restoration project. A "stealth lawn" layout lets tall,
soil-building cover crops grow while still reading as maintained.

## Why this works

Code enforcement typically looks for signs of neglect: tall weeds creeping
over sidewalks/roads, blocked sightlines, or growth swallowing signs. A
crisp mowed border and visible paths immediately signal active maintenance
rather than an abandoned lot.

## The technique

- **Mow a clean, low border** (mower's lowest or near-lowest setting) all the
  way around property lines, sidewalks, and street edges.
- **Cut a few clean walking paths** directly through the interior of the
  cover-crop area (a loop or figure-eight works well). This reads as an
  intentional meadow/walking garden rather than an abandoned lot, and lets
  you walk the space comfortably.
- **Let the interior grow** to the cover crop's natural height between mows.
- **Choose species that tolerate mowing** without dying before they've done
  their soil work, or that naturally stay low/horizontal (see
  summer-cover-crop-comparison-millet-cowpeas-peanut for species options).
- Clean, visible paths and mowed edges also reduce the "creatures moving in"
  concern neighbors often raise about dense growth — open sightlines and
  airflow through the paths discourage rodents from nesting, while the
  interior still supports pollinators and wildlife.

## Practical prep tip

Before broadcasting seed on hydrophobic (water-repelling) sandy soil, a
light spray of biodegradable dish soap breaks surface tension so water and
seed-bed moisture can penetrate evenly, producing a uniform stand rather than
patchy, messy clumps that look unmanaged.
