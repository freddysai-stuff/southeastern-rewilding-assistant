# Drip Irrigation & Timer Schedules for Broadcasting Seed in Narrow Beds

Beds tight against a house foundation sit in a "rain shadow" (the roof
overhang blocks natural rainfall), so consistent irrigation is critical
when broadcasting seed rather than watering individual transplants. This
covers both the physical drip layout and the timer program for two
distinct phases of a planting: germination and deep-root establishment. For
general dry-spell watering without a drip system, see
`dry-weather-irrigation-for-cover-crop-establishment`.

## Two-line drip grid for a narrow (2 ft wide) bed

Standard single-line drip layouts leave dry dead zones when you're
broadcasting seed across the whole surface rather than watering individual
plants. For even coverage across a 24-inch-wide bed:

- Use 1/2-inch inline emitter tubing (built-in pressure-compensating
  emitters every 12 inches, 0.6-0.9 GPH flow rate).
- Lay **two parallel lines** per bed: Line 1 six inches from the back
  wall, Line 2 six inches from the front edge - leaving 12 inches between
  them.
- **Stagger the emitters** so Line 2's emitter holes sit at the midpoint
  between Line 1's holes. This creates a diagonal wetting pattern; as water
  soaks in, wet spots expand and overlap into a solid moisture blanket
  across the topsoil.
- Install a 25 PSI pressure regulator at the faucet/valve - drip tubing
  blows apart or leaks on unregulated household water pressure.
- Footage estimate: beds x 2 lines x bed length (e.g. three 30 ft beds x 2
  lines = 180 ft of emitter tubing).

## Timer schedule — precision drip timer

**Phase 1, Germination (days 1-14):** goal is keeping the top inch of soil
consistently damp so seed doesn't dry out after cracking open. Run 3x/day
(e.g. 8 AM, 12 PM, 4 PM), 5-7 minutes per zone - enough to lightly saturate
the surface without pooling or washing tiny seed away.

**Phase 2, Establishment (day 15 onward through summer):** goal is forcing
roots down away from the hot surface. Switch to once every other day (or
3x/week), early morning (~6 AM), for a much longer 20-30 minute deep soak.

**Fall transition (when overseeding a chopped-down summer planting):**
temporarily switch back to Phase 1 (short, frequent) for about a week to
kick-start winter pea/radish germination, since fall can be surprisingly
dry - then taper off once green sprouts appear, as coastal Georgia winters
usually provide enough rain/dew on their own.

## Timer schedule — basic interval timer (runs every X hours for X minutes, no clock/phase logic)

If your timer only supports a repeating interval rather than scheduled
times of day, these settings replicate the same two-phase logic:

| Phase | Interval | Duration | Start time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summer germination | Every 6 hours | 4 minutes | 9:00 AM (yields 9AM/3PM/9PM/3AM cycles) |
| Summer deep-root establishment (after ~2 weeks, once seedlings are ~2 in tall) | Every 48 hours | 20 minutes | 6:00 AM |
| Fall germination (first 7 days after overseeding) | Every 12 hours | 5 minutes | 8:00 AM (yields 8AM/8PM) |
| Fall/winter maintenance (after sprouting) | Every 72 hours (or off, if regular rain) | 25 minutes | - |

**Why the summer germination timing works:** starting at 9 AM lands
watering cycles at 9AM and 3PM - the hottest, highest-evaporation hours of
the day - while the short 4-minute duration avoids washing away tiny
broadcast seed or rotting it.

**Why the deep-root switch matters:** if germination-phase frequent
watering continues after seedlings establish, roots stay shallow and
plants struggle in real heat. The 48-hour gap forces new roots to grow
downward searching for moisture, building a genuinely heat-resistant bed.
