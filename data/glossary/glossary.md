# Glossary

**Rewilding** — Restoring a plot of land to a self-sustaining, native
ecosystem by re-introducing native plants and reducing artificial inputs.

**Native plant** — A species that occurs naturally in a region without human
introduction, adapted to local soil, climate, and wildlife relationships.

**Bloom window** — The range of months during which a plant is expected to
flower, used for pollinator succession planning.

**Compaction** — Degree to which soil particles have been pressed together,
reducing pore space, water infiltration, and root penetration. Common in
former turf lawns.

**Organic matter** — The decomposed plant/animal material in soil that
improves structure, water retention, and microbial activity.

**Companion planting** — Growing species together that benefit each other
(e.g., pest deterrence, pollinator support, structural support).

**Cover crop** — A crop planted primarily to improve soil health
(e.g., suppress weeds, add organic matter, fix nitrogen) rather than for
harvest.

**Corridor** — A planted strip or pathway that connects habitat patches,
allowing pollinators and wildlife to move safely between them.
