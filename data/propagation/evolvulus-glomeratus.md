# Propagating Blue Daze (Evolvulus glomeratus) by Stem Layering

1. Grow the mother plant in a wide, shallow-filled pot: leave 2-3 in. of
   headspace between the soil line and the pot rim rather than filling to
   the top. The deeper rim traps humidity at the soil surface, encouraging
   trailing stems to root faster wherever they touch down.
2. As stems sprawl and crawl across the soil surface, their nodes will
   naturally want to root on contact with damp dirt.
3. To force it deliberately, press a small handful of soil over the middle
   of a long trailing stem, leaving the leafy tip exposed above the surface.
   Pin the buried section down with a scrap of wire, a hairpin, or repurposed
   plastic irrigation fitting so it stays in firm soil contact.
4. Within about 2 weeks, the buried node sprouts its own roots, creating a
   pre-rooted plug still attached to the mother plant.
5. Once rooted, snip the connecting stem to separate the new plug and pot it
   up independently, or leave it in place to thicken the mother planting.

Because Blue Daze spreads so readily this way, a single 1-gallon nursery
plant given its own wide pot (with generous headspace) can produce multiple
free plugs by the following spring with no cuttings or rooting hormone
required.
