# Propagating Golden Shrimp Plant (Justicia brandegeeana) by Stem Cutting or Air-Layering

## Stem cuttings (windowsill method)

1. Best timing: late summer to early autumn (August-September), at least a
   month before the first expected frost - stems are mature and root faster
   than soft spring growth, and rooted cuttings double as winter backups for
   a plant that cannot survive freezing outdoors.
2. Choose a healthy green stem without an active yellow shrimp-like bloom
   (snip any bloom off first so the cutting isn't wasting energy flowering).
3. Cut a 4-6 in. piece just below a node (the small bump where leaves emerge).
4. Strip leaves off the bottom half; leave 2-4 leaves at the top.
5. Root in a clear glass of water on a bright windowsill (change water every
   few days; white roots appear in ~2 weeks) or push the bare end directly
   into a small pot of damp potting mix and keep consistently moist.
6. Once water-rooted cuttings have ~1 in. roots, pot them up in a small
   plastic pot with a standard soil/compost mix.

## Air-layering (no cutting needed, done in the pot)

1. Do not clip the top flower on the mother plant. Instead, gently bend the
   tall, flexible stem sideways so the mid-section arches down and rests
   flat against moist soil inside a wide pot.
2. Pin the bent section firmly to the soil (a scrap of stiff wire or plastic
   works fine).
3. Within 2-3 weeks, the pinned section sprouts its own roots directly into
   the soil.
4. Snip the stem just behind the new root mass to separate a fully rooted
   plant without ever managing a water jar.

Winter care for new plants: keep near the brightest indoor window, water
only when the top inch of soil is dry, and expect slow growth through the
dark months - they'll be ready to move outside again next spring.
